ORIGO: Deep-Time Ancestral Atlas

An in-browser bioinformatics engine and 4D geospatial atlas that transforms raw consumer genomic variant files into an interactive chronicle tracking 300,000 years of human evolutionary dispersal.

Scientific Vision & In-Browser Genomics

Traditional commercial genetic genealogy presents ancestry as static, flat pie charts and isolated regional percentages (e.g., "42% Northwestern European"). ORIGO transforms raw consumer genomic data into a four-dimensional journey across 300,000 years of evolutionary history, charting ancestral survival corridors from archaic speciation in Africa through the Last Glacial Maximum and Neolithic agricultural expansions into the modern era.

Genomic variant arrays contain intensely sensitive, unchangeable health and identity data. ORIGO is architected so that raw files are decompressed, parsed, and queried completely inside browser memory using pako and typed arrays. No genomic data is ever uploaded to a remote server.

Core Subsystems & Scientific Pipeline

1. Universal Genomic Parser (`universalParser.ts`)

Detects, decompresses, and normalizes disparate raw DNA formats directly in client RAM:

  • 23andMe: Build 37 and Build 38 tab-delimited formats.
  • AncestryDNA: Commented TSV/CSV format with multi-allele parsing.
  • Standardized VCF / gVCF: In-memory gzip decompression via pako.
  • Normalization Pipeline: Extracts rsID, chromosome, position, and genotype into indexed typed arrays for instant O(1) hash lookups.

2. Phylogenetic & Admixture Engine

Resolves paternal Y-DNA haplogroups (A00 down through R1b-M269, I1-M253, E1b1b, J2, and Q-M242) and maternal mtDNA (L0 through H, U, K, J, T, and Indigenous American clades). Incorporates an Archaic Introgression Resolver that screens against ancient Vindija Neanderthal and Altai Denisovan genomes to compute inherited archaic traits (such as high-altitude EPAS1 mutations).

3. Interactive 4D WebGL Migration Globe

Powered by Three.js and React Three Fiber, the 3D globe renders geological ice sheet overlays, continental shelf exposures during glacial maximums, and dynamic geodesic migration arcs. Players scrub through 4D time slices from 300,000 BCE to 1900 CE.

4. Evidence Workbench & Archaeological Benchmarking

Every historical migration vector is grounded in peer-reviewed archaeogenetics. The Evidence Workbench links genetic mutations directly to radiocarbon-dated excavation sites and landmark papers published in Nature, Science, and Cell via persistent DOI links.
